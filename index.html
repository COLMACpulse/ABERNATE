Two engineers. Six months. It has never run.

That is the money. Not a saving, not a projection. A finished system, already on the books, held at the last step by a CISO who will not put her name to it.

She is right to hesitate. When the board asks "was that action authorized?" she has a log file and an opinion. A signed receipt for every action, naming the policy that allowed it and the human who cleared it, turns her approval from a judgement call she owns personally into a document she can produce.

Three other lines show up in the business case and none of them are ours to quantify. EU AI Act exposure runs to €35 million or 7% of turnover, and Finland has been enforcing since January 2026. SOC 2 and ISO evidence is assembled by people at consulting rates, and here it is produced per action at no cost. And when the refund agent does issue the wrong refund, proving what was authorized is the difference between a contained incident and the company eating the loss. Nobody has measured a dollar of this yet, so we are not going to print one.

Nobody can answer "was that allowed?"

An agent deletes a customer record. Three weeks later the customer's lawyer asks why, and a support lead has to answer. The logs show the deletion happened. They do not show which policy allowed it, what credentials it ran under, or whether anyone on staff approved it.

Most governance tools settle this at deploy time, in a config review the platform team signed off in March. That decision was made once, against permissions, staff and data that have all changed since.

ABERNATE decides at the action. Every step, every time.

Four things must agree, or it does not happen

Permission is not one setting. Before an action runs, four separate questions have to answer yes.

01Can this worker do this at all? Its contract lists the actions it has and the paths it may read.
02Does the command being run permit it? A command is a narrower grant than the worker's full capability.
03Does standing policy allow it? Risk class, network access, evidence thresholds, step limits.
04Did this job actually ask for it? An action nobody requested is refused even when all three above allow it.
ALL FOUR, OR NOTHING  ·  five strong properties and one dead one is a refusal, and the dead one gets named. Nothing averages out.

A named human has the last word and can only say no. Their GO cannot push an action through a check that already failed, and a policy written without that gate is rejected the moment it loads, so no engineer disables it by forgetting it.

Every step writes a receipt, hash-linked to the one before it and signed. That file goes to the customer's lawyer, the SOC 2 auditor or opposing counsel. Each checks it with a command that ships in the package. None has to trust the operator, and none has to call us.

Three things not found elsewhere

  • It refuses on the weakest link, never the average Scoring systems let a strong result cover a bad one. Here a single failed condition stops the action and the output names which one. There is no total high enough to pass a refusal.
  • It ships the record of its own defeats Ten forgeries were run against its evidence chain and all ten are caught. One of them was disclosed for months as structurally unfixable, and is now closed. Each is a passing test, so none can be quietly dropped. They are ten attacks this team thought of; an attack nobody here thought of is not in that number. The list, the method, and the record of which ones got through first, ship with the packet.
  • Three earlier versions had a flagship claim measured false Each was found by attacking the claim rather than restating it, repaired, and proven by putting the defect back and watching the test fail. Those records ship with the package instead of being cleaned out of it.

What it will not do

  • It does not protect an organization from its own key holders Receipts are signed, so the customer, the auditor and opposing counsel cannot forge one. The security team holding the signing key still can. That is what a signature means, and it turns "could this have been faked" into "who held the key on 14 March", which access records already answer.
  • It does not prove the decision was right It proves the action followed the authorities that governed it: which policy allowed it, under what capability, and who cleared it. Whether holding that document was legally correct is a judgement a lawyer makes, and no machine on this page makes it. Anyone who tells a court otherwise will be taken apart on it.
  • It is not an agent framework and does not replace one It sits underneath whatever framework the agents are built with, and rules on the actions they take.
  • Receipt hashes do not survive a move between machines Runs are deterministic and reproduce identical hashes at a fixed path. Move the folder and the paths inside the receipts change, so the hashes change with them. Named in the technical brief rather than discovered during evaluation.
